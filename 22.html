    Slides 2–3 · Problem Q1

    Inertia tensor & angular momentum of 3 point masses

    Three point masses m1 = 2 kg at (1, −1, 1), m2 = 1 kg at (2, 0, 2), and m3 = 4 kg at (−1, 1, 0) are joined by massless rods and rotate with ω = 3i − 2j + 4k rad/s.

    Step-by-step calculation (Slides 2–3)

    1. 01
      Ixx = ∑ mi(yi2 + zi2) = 2(1 + 1) + 1(0 + 4) + 4(1 + 0) = 12,   Iyy = 16,   Izz = 16
      Diagonal moments of inertia sum squared perpendicular distances (in kg·m2).
    2. 02
      Ixy = −∑ mixiyi = −[2(−1) + 1(0) + 4(−1)] = 6,   Ixz = −6,   Iyz = 2
      Off-diagonal products of inertia are symmetric (Iij = Iji).
    3. 03
      L = [I]ω = 126−66162−62163−24= 0−642 kg·m2/s
      Lx = 36 − 12 − 24 = 0; Ly = 18 − 32 + 8 = −6; Lz = −18 − 4 + 64 = 42. Notice L is not parallel to ω!

    Slides 4–13 · Two-Step Method

    Physical meaning of eigenvectors & diagonalization

    Step 1 finds the directions where L = λω by solving det([I] − λ[1]) = 0. Step 2 rotates the coordinate axes onto those eigenvectors so the inertia tensor becomes purely diagonal: D = diag(λ1, λ2).

    1. 01

      Slides 6–7: non-trivial ω requires the determinant to vanish.
    2. 02
      λ1 = 1 ⟹ ωx = ωy  (45°),   λ2 = 3 ⟹ ωx = −ωy  (135°)
      Slides 8–10: the two eigenvectors are mutually perpendicular.
    3. 03
      [I]principal = D = 1003,   L1 = λ1ω1,  L2 = λ2ω2
      Slides 11–13: rotating the coordinate axes to coincide with the eigenvectors diagonalizes [I] and uncouples the angular momentum equations.
    Slides 14–22 · Rotating Dumbbell

    Principal axes of a 45° rotating dumbbell

    Two point masses m at distance 2b from the origin lie in the xy-plane along the 135° / −45° line (y = −x), at (−√2b, √2b, 0) and (√2b, −√2b, 0).

    Eigenvalues & eigenvectors of the dumbbell (Slides 16–22)

    1. 01
      [I] = m2b22b202b22b20004b2= 2mb2110110002
      Slide 16: since xiyi = −2b2 for each mass, Ixy = −∑ mixiyi = +4mb2 (or 2mb2 for each mass at r = √2b).
    2. 02
      det([I] − λ[1]) = 0  ⟹  (4mb2 − λ) · λ · (λ − 4mb2) = 0  ⟹  λ = 4mb2, 4mb2, 0
      Slides 17–19: two equal non-zero principal moments (4mb2) and one zero principal moment (0).
    3. 03
      λ1 = 4mb2: (1, 1, 0);   λ2 = 4mb2: (0, 0, 1);   λ3 = 0: (1, −1, 0)
      Slides 20–22: λ3 = 0 is along the rod connecting the point masses (no moment of inertia about a line of point masses); λ1, λ2 are perpendicular to the rod.

    Slides 23–27 · Degenerate Eigenvalues

    3 equal masses & a plane of principal axes

    Three equal point masses m sit at (a, 0, 0), (0, a, 2a), and (0, 2a, a). Because two eigenvalues are equal (λ = 10ma2, 10ma2, 2ma2), the eigenvectors for 10ma2 span an entire 2D plane: (α, 1, −1) for any α!

    Finding the principal moments & plane of axes (Slides 24–27)

    1. 01
      [I] = ma2100006−40−46
      Ixx = m(0 + 5a2 + 5a2) = 10ma2; Iyy = m(a2 + 4a2 + a2) = 6ma2; Iyz = −m(0 + 2a2 + 2a2) = −4ma2.
    2. 02
      (10ma2 − λ)[(6ma2 − λ)2 − 16m2a4] = 0  ⟹  λ = 10ma2, 10ma2, 2ma2
      Slide 24: (6 − λ)2 = 16 ⟹ 6 − λ = ±4 ⟹ λ = 2ma2 or 10ma2. Thus 10ma2 is a repeated root!
    3. 03
      λ = 10ma2:  −4ωy − 4ωz = 0  ⟹  ωy = −ωz,  ωx = α (free)  ⟹  (α, 1, −1)
      Slides 25–26: any vector in the plane y + z = 0 is a principal axis! Choosing two orthogonal directions in this plane gives (1, 0, 0) and (0, 1, −1)/√2.
    4. 04
      λ = 2ma2:  8ωx = 0,  4ωy − 4ωz = 0  ⟹  (0, 1, 1)/√2
      Slide 27: the third orthogonal principal axis is (0, 1, 1)/√2, perpendicular to the degenerate plane, giving D = diag(10, 10, 2)ma2.

    Slides 28–29 · König's / CM Decomposition

    Show that Lz = I0ω + (R × MV)z

    The total angular momentum of a translating and rotating rigid body separates cleanly into the orbital angular momentum of the center of mass R × MV plus the spin angular momentum about the center of mass I0ω.

    Proof of L = R × MV + I0ω (Slides 28–29)

    1. 01
      ri = R + ri′,   vi = V + vi′
      Slide 28: express each particle's lab position and velocity as CM motion (R, V) plus motion relative to the CM (ri′, vi′).
    2. 02
      L = ∑ (R + ri′) × mi(V + vi′) = R × MV + R × (∑ mivi′) + (∑ miri′) × V + ∑ ri′ × mivi′
      Slide 29: expand the cross product into four sums.
    3. 03
      ∑ miri′ = 0  and  ∑ mivi′ = 0
      Slide 29: because ri′ is measured from the center of mass, both middle cross-terms vanish identically!
    4. 04
      L = R × MV + I0ω  ⟹  Lz = I0ω + (R × MV)z
      Slide 29: the remaining relative sum ∑ ri′ × mivi′ = I0ω is the rotational angular momentum about the CM.
