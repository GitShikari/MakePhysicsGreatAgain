    Expanding L = Σ rj × mj(ω × rj)

    When a rigid body rotates with an arbitrary 3D angular velocity ω = ωxx + ωyy + ωzz, expanding the double cross product reveals why Lx depends on all three components of ω.

    Double cross-product geometry for particle j

    I_xx = m(y² + z²)

    −0.80 kg·m²

    −1.00 kg·m²
    L_x = I_xx ω_x + I_xy ω_y + I_xz ω_z
    −0.28

    Step-by-step determinant expansion (Slides 3–5)

    1. 01
      pj = mjdrjdt = mj(ω × rj)  ⟹  L = Σj=1N [rj × mj(ω × rj)]
      Every particle j has velocity vj = ω × rj, where ω = ωxx + ωyy + ωzz.
    2. 02
      ω × rj = xyzωxωyωzxjyjzj = (ωyzj − yjωz)x − (ωxzj − xjωz)y + (ωxyj − xjωy)z
      First evaluate the inner cross product ω × rj using the 3×3 determinant on Slide 3.
    3. 03
      rj × mj(ω × rj) = xyzxjyjzjmj(ωyzj−yjωz)−mj(ωxzj−xjωz)mj(ωxyj−xjωy)
      Now cross rj into mj(ω × rj) (Slide 4 top determinant) and collect coefficients of ωx, ωy, ωz in the x component.
    4. 04
      Lx = Σj=1N [mj(yj2 + zj2)ωx − mjxjyjωy − mjxjzjωz]
      Notice that Lx receives contributions not only from ωx, but also from ωy and ωz!
    5. 05
      Lx = Ixxωx + Ixyωy + Ixzωz
      Slide 5 definition: Introduce the diagonal moment of inertia Ixx = Σ mj(yj2 + zj2) and the off-diagonal products of inertia Ixy = −Σ mjxjyj and Ixz = −Σ mjxjzj.

    The Matrix Equation: [L] = [I][ω] & [τ] = [I][α]

    The fact that [I] is a 3×3 matrix means that L and ω do not necessarily point in the same direction — and torque τ and angular acceleration α may not point in the same direction either!

    Slides 6–8: Unlike p = mv (where mass m is a scalar so p ∥ v), moment of inertia [I] is a 3×3 matrix. Therefore L about one axis depends on ω about all three axes!

    Diagonal vs. off-diagonal elements of [I]

    Diagonal integrands (y2+z2, z2+x2, x2+y2) are the squared perpendicular distances to the x, y, z axes. Off-diagonal elements (−∫ xy dm, etc.) measure mass imbalance across quadrants.

    −2.40 kg·m²

    Mass distribution
    IMBALANCED
    [I] = ∫(y2 + z2)dm −∫ xy dm −∫ zx dm −∫ xy dm ∫(z2 + x2)dm −∫ yz dm −∫ zx dm −∫ yz dm ∫(x2 + y2)dm
    Slide 9–10 continuous medium matrix: Notice that [I] is always symmetric (Ixy = Iyx, Ixz = Izx, Iyz = Izy).
    Why reflection symmetry kills off-diagonal terms. Press Symmetric 4-mass quad above: for every mass at (+x, +y), there is an equal mirror mass at (−x, +y). Their xy products have opposite signs and cancel to zero!

    Principal axes & matrix diagonalization

    For any rigid body, there exist three mutually perpendicular axes passing through the body about which all products of inertia (Ixy, Iyz, Izx) vanish. Rotate the coordinate frame below until [I] becomes diagonal!

    2.12 kg·m²
    I_y'y' (Moment 2)
    4.88 kg·m²
    Off-diagonal I_x'y'
    −1.80 kg·m²
    Is L parallel to ω?
    NO (Cumbersome!)

    In arbitrary axes (Slide 13 top — "Cumbersome!")

    Lx = Ixxωx + Ixyωy + Ixzωz Ly = Iyxωx + Iyyωy + Iyzωz Lz = Izxωx + Izyωy + Izzωz
    All three axes are coupled together by 6 off-diagonal terms.

    In Principal Axes (Slide 13 bottom — Diagonal!)

    [I] = I1000I2000I3
    Lx = I1ωx,  Ly = I2ωy,  Lz = I3ωz
    Found via matrix diagonalization: det(I − λ1) = 0, where eigenvalues λ = (I1, I2, I3) are the principal moments of inertia!

    How Vectors and Rank-2 Tensors transform under rotation

    A physical quantity is only a true tensor if its components transform by the rotation matrix Rij when you rotate your coordinate axes: once for a Rank-1 vector (F′i = RijFj), and twice for a Rank-2 tensor (m′ij = RikRjlmkl).

    Live coordinate rotation calculator: F′i = RijFj & I′ij = RikRjlIkl

    Rank 0: T' = T
    300 K (invariant)
    Rank 1: F'_i = R_ij F_j
    (2.00, 1.00, 0.00)
    Rank 2: I'_xx, I'_yy
    3.00, 3.00
    Rank 2: I'_xy = R_ik R_jl I_kl
    −1.00 (at 45° → 0.00!)

    Rank-1 Vector Transformation (Slide 18)

    F′i = Σj Rij Fj
    One index i → one copy of the rotation matrix R.

    Rank-2 Tensor Transformation (Slide 19)

    m′ij = Σk,l Rik Rjl mkl
    Two indices i, j → two copies of the rotation matrix (M′ = R M RT).

    Every equation and tensor definition from 20.pptx (Slides 1–22), linked to its interactive visualizer.

    General 3D Angular Momentum

    L = Σj=1N [rj × mj(ω × rj)]
    Where ω = ωxx + ωyy + ωzz.

    Diagonal Moments of Inertia

    Ixx = ∫(y2 + z2)dm
    Similarly Iyy = ∫(z2+x2)dm, Izz = ∫(x2+y2)dm.

    Off-Diagonal Products of Inertia

    Ixy = Iyx = −∫ xy dm
    Measure of mass imbalance; vanishes for symmetric axes.

    Matrix Equation for L

    [L] = [I][ω],   Lx = Ixxωx + Ixyωy + Ixzωz
    L and ω do not necessarily point in the same direction.

    Matrix Equation for Torque τ

    [τ] = [I][α],   τz = Izxαx + Izyαy + Izzαz
    Torque and angular acceleration α may not point in the same direction.

    Principal Axes & Eigenvalues

    det(I − λ1) = 0  ⟹  Li = Iiωi
    Along principal axes, [I] is diagonal and L ∥ ω.

    Rank-1 Vector Rotation

    F′i = Rij Fj
    1 index → Rank-1 tensor.

    Rank-2 Tensor Rotation

    m′ij = Rik Rjl mkl
    2 indices (bi-directional coupling) → Rank-2 tensor.
